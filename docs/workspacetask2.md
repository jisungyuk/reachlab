standardize the starting position of the wrist
in our prior study, starting position was with the wrist at a height level with the xiphoid process, directly in line with the nose (when facing perfectly forward), and elbow at 90 degrees (I have a goniometer you can use to quickly position elbow properly)
what if the elbow won't go to 90 degrees?  e.g., it is tight and therefore more flexed?  Well then we can make an exception for those participants: at baseline set the elbow angle at whatever degree of extension it will passively stay at.  Measure that angle.  Set them up at that same angle at all subsequent outcome assessments. 
standardize the seating position of the participant relative to the table and to the chair (with strapping).  We'll need to get an appropriate chair.
the moving target needs to become visible and start moving from a position that makes sense with the above starting wrist position, e.g., the target should start directly in front of the patient (12 o'clock position) just slightly beyond their reach, requiring them to try to reach straight ahead.  Then from there, the target should sweep laterally to the 3 o'clock position (if testing the right side), then change directions like a windshield wiper and sweep medially through 12 o'clock all the way to 9 o'clock, then back through 12 o'clock  to 3 o'clock, then back to 9 o'clock, and then to 12 o'clock completing one trial.  I think repeating back and forth in a single trial is necessary to give the patient a chance to get used to reaching.  The final calculation for the trial should be a measure of the largest envelope they were able to reach to over the entire trial.
An alternative target path is to not have the target change directions like a windshield wiper, but instead move in a continuous oval, 3 times around the oval for a single trial.  In this case at least two trials would be needed; one with the target moving in the counterclockwise direction, and one with the target moving in the clockwise direction.  The final calculation would be the largest envelope they trace out over the two trials.
The target should move at a rate that corresponds to ~90 seconds (?) to complete the whole trial - that what we did in prior study, with the ability of the assessor to adjust that speed if needed for participants.
